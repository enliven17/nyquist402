title: History & recovery
description: Recover payment history, reconcile pending transactions and preserve the original agreement.

## Reconnect to a payment

Connect the participant wallet and sign in to retrieve private payment history and case material. A buyer or seller can resume its permitted work from another browser. Access to evidence follows the payment participants and current arbiter.

A payment ID lets you identify and read public contract state. It does not grant a role, private evidence access or signing authority. The ledger remains authoritative for balances, claim status, deadlines and ruling authority.

## Resolve a pending transaction

Keep the transaction hash returned for a submission. If confirmation is delayed or the connection drops, look up that original transaction before preparing a replacement.

- **Confirmed:** read the resulting payment or claim state and continue from that outcome.
- **Failed:** inspect the failure, refresh state and prepare an eligible action again.
- **Still pending or unknown:** continue reconciliation; do not assume the action failed because a response was lost.

Payment history retains submission identities so reconnecting does not require guessing whether a claim or payout happened. A saved payment ID or signed transaction is not proof of confirmed funding.

## Recover unused funds

After session expiry, the buyer can reclaim unused principal and eligible fee reserves. Pending claims retain the principal and arbitration capacity they need. Session expiry does not finalize an outstanding dispute or release another claim's reservation.

Acceptance, settlement, a valid ruling or terminal fallback finalizes each claim independently. Refresh state and check confirmation before repeating any action.

## Archived contract records

An archived entry, a missing payment and a delayed history record are different conditions. An archived entry needs its Soroban storage restored before the original action can be retried; its agreement and funds are not redefined by that process.

## Automatic restoration

**Not fully implemented yet.** The planned recovery flow is explicit and separate from the business action:

1. Simulation identifies archived entries and returns `restoration_required` with the affected footprint, estimated network fee, current ledger and a restoration transaction template. It does not report the payment as nonexistent.
2. The SDK checks that the footprint is limited to the configured contract instance/code, required payment/claim records and authentication dependencies for the requested action. Unexpected addresses or keys stop recovery for inspection. Restoration is allowed only within the caller’s explicit fee budget.
3. The caller signs the restoration transaction as its fee source. A managed worker may sponsor it only from its configured restoration budget; sponsorship never authorizes an escrow payout. Broadcast uses its own idempotent operation and persisted hash.
4. Wait for ledger-confirmed restoration, then read current terms, claim state, revision and authority again. Reconcile any original pending business transaction before deciding whether a new action is needed.
5. Simulate and prepare the still-eligible original action again, collecting fresh role and envelope signatures. Restoring storage cannot preserve an expired ruling window or revive a finalized claim.

A restoration preparation expires after 120 seconds and must be re-simulated if its fee estimate or footprint changes. The managed default restoration budget is 1 XLM per transaction and 10 XLM per payment per UTC day; exhaustion produces a blocked job, `RESTORATION_UNAVAILABLE` and an operator alert. A direct caller may explicitly choose a different fee budget without changing the protected agreement.

Storage maintenance checks live instance, code, payment and claim TTL every hour. It targets retention through the maximum remaining signed lifecycle plus a 7-day buffer, capped by the network’s permitted TTL. If the network cap is shorter, it renews in bounded increments and alerts when fewer than 48 hours remain. It never assumes a single TTL extension covers a long-running agreement.

All restoration hashes, fees and outcomes appear in payment activity. A failed restore is not a failed claim; a successful restore is not a successful payout. [API transaction states](/docs/reference/service-api#idempotency-and-interrupted-requests) apply independently to each operation.

## Continue during a service outage

Use the payment's original contract and network to read state and submit eligible actions directly through the [client](/docs/testnet/sdk). Acceptance, disputes and rulings retain their role requirements. Settlement after review and terminal fallback remain publicly callable when eligible.

Private evidence still needs to be retrieved through its authorized delivery mechanism. Managed retention and outage recovery guarantees are [not fully implemented yet](/docs/services/evidence#retention-and-confidentiality); chain restoration alone cannot recover a missing document.
