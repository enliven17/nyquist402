**Managed operations are not fully implemented yet.** The numbers below are proposed launch defaults and acceptance targets for the managed service. They define the intended operating policy; they are not measurements or guarantees of the current testnet demo.

## Service targets

| Measure | Planned target | Measurement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| API availability | 99.9% per UTC calendar month | Eligible requests completing without a service-caused error; planned maintenance counts as downtime |
| State and evidence reads | p95 under 2 seconds | Authenticated request arrival to full response for records within documented limits |
| Mutation acknowledgment | p95 under 5 seconds | Valid request to a durable operation identity or explicit rejection; not blockchain confirmation |
| Due-action submission | p95 within 30 seconds | Contract eligibility to first broadcast when ledger/RPC are healthy and the configured fee budget is available |
| Index freshness | At most 3 ledgers behind | Current network ledger minus the last completely indexed ledger |
| Incident acknowledgment | Within 15 minutes, 24/7 | Critical alert delivery to an identified operator acknowledging ownership |
| Durable-data recovery point | At most 5 minutes | Maximum acknowledged metadata/evidence loss in a disaster restoration exercise |
| Service recovery time | Within 60 minutes | Disaster declared to a reconciled service accepting normal traffic |

Availability excludes client-caused 4xx responses. Rate limiting within the documented allowance, failed authorization due to a service defect and dependency failures that prevent an eligible request all count against availability. Execution latency is reported separately during network outages; deadlines and private evidence obligations do not pause. Blockchain confirmation time and arbiters' merits decisions are not controlled by an API latency target.

Three-region synthetic probes run every minute. The monthly report includes request success rates, p50/p95/p99 latency, index lag, overdue actions, incidents and recovery results; it reports both overall and dependency-attributed failures. A five-minute availability failure, index lag above 3 ledgers for 60 seconds, or an eligible action waiting 30 seconds pages the on-call operator.

## Network and release compatibility

`GET /supported` identifies the network, escrow, code hash, asset, default arbiter and supported credential/capability names. The client verifies the contract identity and pins the agreement before signing. Changes to service defaults cannot alter funded terms. See the [public API contract](/docs/reference/service-api) and [testnet setup](/docs/setup).

New service releases are additive within the advertised schema. A breaking request or receipt format uses a new explicitly negotiated schema identifier and is rejected by incompatible clients; no request is silently reinterpreted. Private evidence retrieval and direct-action recovery remain available through the full retention period for funded agreements.

## Signing authority and key controls

Keep relay submission, seller authorization, arbiter ruling and backup access separate. Managed signing keys stay in a hardware-backed signing service; application processes receive permitted signing operations rather than exported private keys. Each signing request records the payment, action, revision and approving role without logging the signature payload or evidence body.

Production deployments require two operators to approve key-policy changes and backup-key recovery. A relay key can be rotated without changing the contract's role identities. An arbiter identity pinned to a funded agreement is never replaced by an operational key change; if its signing authority cannot be recovered, the original escalation and fallback windows apply.

## Execution and reconciliation

Workers scan due actions every 5 seconds and take a durable lease keyed by network, contract, payment, claim and action. A lease expires after 30 seconds and is renewed every 10 seconds while a worker is active. The job record outlives the lease and stores the exact signed XDR and hash before broadcast.

A replacement worker first reconciles that transaction. It may rebroadcast the same still-valid XDR; it cannot generate a competing payout because a lease expired. After conclusive failure or expiry, it refreshes the claim and prepares only an action still eligible under the funded agreement. State checks happen again at contract execution.

Unknown outcomes alert after 60 seconds and remain visible until resolved. Insufficient fee budget, unavailable RPC, archived state and missed eligibility windows each have a distinct job status and alert reason. The worker never shortens a review window or converts an active dispute into an undisputed settlement.

## Backups and restoration

Acknowledged evidence writes must reach two independent storage copies before success is returned. Metadata is replicated continuously and checkpointed at least every 5 minutes. Encrypted daily snapshots and point-in-time logs are retained for 30 days in a separate failure domain. Access to backups is logged and restricted to the recovery role.

Run a restoration exercise monthly. Restore metadata and immutable bodies, verify their hashes, rebuild chain-derived state from the last durable checkpoint, and reconcile every pending transaction before enabling submission. A degraded service exposes its last checked ledger and blocks new financial mutations until reconciliation completes.

Every incident has one owner. The public status record is updated within 30 minutes of confirmation and at least every 30 minutes while material user impact continues. A post-incident report is published within 5 business days and identifies impact, recovery actions and prevention work without exposing private case data.

## Arbitration operations

The Nyquist402 default is a project-operated arbitration service. Its [signed provider manifest](/docs/services/discovery) identifies its operator, ruling account, scope, fees, evidence policy and response commitments. Directory listing is identity and compatibility information, not a claim of impartiality or endorsement.

A case is assigned to an eligible reviewer after conflict screening and evidence access checks. Reviewers must recuse for a direct financial, employment or personal relationship with either party; the conflict and reassignment are recorded. Reassignment is internal to the same pinned ruling identity and cannot extend a contract deadline or create a new arbiter.

If a conflict cannot be resolved within the assigned window, the provider does not invent a ruling or change the agreement. It reports inability to handle the case and allows the pre-agreed escalation or fallback. Late provider responses and completed rulings are not appeals against finalized outcomes.

## Mainnet activation

Mainnet activation requires independent review of allocation, signing, quotes, evidence access and recovery; deployment/code-hash verification; a successful restoration exercise; and 14 consecutive days of measured target performance. Any critical or high-severity review finding blocks activation until remediation is verified. The service capability record advertises a network only after these checks; no client may infer mainnet support from testnet availability.
