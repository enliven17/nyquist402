## Network and payment compatibility

Every payment identifies its network, escrow contract and agreement version. Clients and services check that identity before accepting funding or submitting lifecycle actions. Zero-fee offers use the same protected settlement rules; the accepted agreement determines the charges.

Service releases will publish supported networks, contract addresses, code hashes, SDK versions and API schemas. The [testnet setup guide](/docs/setup) lists the test network and escrow versions used for evaluation.

## Role authority and keys

Keep transaction submission, seller authorization and arbiter ruling authority separate. A facilitator may pay network fees and relay a signed call; that does not let it spend the work budget or decide a case.

Sellers control authorization for their claims. Arbiters sign only cases assigned to them and only within the accepted ruling window. Key rotation or service recovery cannot substitute a different arbiter for one pinned to an existing agreement.

The arbitration service's operating policy will define assignment, conflicts of interest, escalation and audit procedures.

## Monitor the payment lifecycle

Monitor pending transactions, eligible actions awaiting execution, case deadlines and evidence retrieval. Reconcile payment history with onchain state and track principal, reserved fees, earned fees and returns separately.

An interface must distinguish delayed indexing, pending confirmation and a failed transaction. Evidence retrieval failures must remain visible to participants reviewing a case.

## Recover interrupted work

Recover the original transaction identity and reconcile its result before resuming a job. Restore private case records with their access permissions and body versions. A restored service must preserve payment terms, deadlines, evidence revisions and finalized outcomes.

If Soroban archives required state, restore that state before preparing the action again. Restoring a contract entry cannot recover a missing offchain evidence body; the two recovery paths have different responsibilities.

## Managed operations — not fully implemented yet

The managed service will publish availability and execution-latency targets, evidence retention and deletion policies, access auditing, backup recovery procedures and incident guidance. Automatic storage maintenance and restoration are also being completed.

Mainnet activation will follow independent security review and deployment verification. See the [service roadmap](/docs/roadmap) for these remaining capabilities and [direct-action recovery](/docs/protected/recovery) for the contract path during a service outage.
