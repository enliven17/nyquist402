## Commitments and bodies

The contract stores minimal delivery and evidence commitments. The intended evidence service stores the corresponding agreement, delivery receipts and case submissions and delivers them to authorized participants. A hash alone neither stores a document nor gives an arbiter access to it.

On retrieval, verify each body against its recorded commitment. Keep the case revision and the exact evidence snapshot visible to the arbiter and parties.

## Access control

Authorize access for the payment's buyer, seller and currently assigned arbiter. Authenticate the requesting account and check its case relationship; a public payment ID is not an access token. Separate public state/history views from private evidence retrieval.

When a case escalates, enable the pinned default to retrieve the evidence required to decide the case, with access changes consistent with the current assignment. Do not expose evidence to unrelated accounts.

## Submission and revisions

An evidence body must remain available when its commitment is submitted. Updates preserve the previous record for audit purposes and bind the current version used by a ruling. A server restart, browser reload or assignment change must not erase the submitted evidence or its access permissions.

## Retention and confidentiality

The service must publish retention, access and deletion behavior and preserve availability throughout the response and ruling windows, including handoff. Deployment documentation specifies retention durations, storage limits and service guarantees.

Do not publish private evidence bodies to the ledger. Operators need restricted storage/key access and access audit records. See [operations](/docs/services/operators).
