## Agreement, delivery and evidence

Nyquist402 keeps agreement text, delivery descriptions and case submissions offchain. The contract records their commitments. Participants retrieve the corresponding bodies through the case service and verify their hashes before relying on them.

A hash alone neither stores a document nor gives an arbiter access to it. Keep the payment, claim and evidence revision attached to the material being reviewed.

## Sign in and retrieve a case

Authenticate with the account that participates in the payment. The service checks that the requesting account is the buyer, seller or currently assigned arbiter before returning private case material. Knowing a public payment ID grants state reads, not private evidence access.

Wallet sign-in uses a signed challenge that is never broadcast. This establishes service identity; it does not authorize a contract transaction.

When authority passes from a custom arbiter to the pinned default, access follows the current assignment so the default can review the same case record. Unrelated accounts cannot retrieve its private bodies.

## Submit and revise evidence

Store the body before submitting its commitment to the contract. During the evidence window, a party may update its submission. Previous body versions remain retained, while the current commitment and revision identify the evidence relevant to a ruling.

A browser reload, service restart or arbitration handoff must preserve the submitted material. The arbiter checks the current revision and body hashes before signing an award.

## Retention and confidentiality

Private evidence stays off the public ledger. Access is tied to the authenticated participant and current case relationship. Transaction authorization remains separate from permission to read a document.

**Managed retention guarantees are not fully implemented yet.** The service will publish retention durations, storage limits, deletion rules and access-audit procedures, covering the response and ruling windows and escalation. These guarantees will explain how case material remains retrievable during recovery and service interruptions.

See [history and recovery](/docs/protected/recovery) for interrupted transactions and [service operations](/docs/services/operators) for continuity requirements.
