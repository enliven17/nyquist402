Nyquist402 lets buyers and software agents fund work before its final cost is known. The buyer and seller agree a budget and settlement rules, the seller records delivery claims, and the buyer reviews each claim before payment. Soroban escrow enforces the agreement; arbitration resolves disputed deliveries.

Start with the [demo walkthrough](/docs/quickstart) to try the flow, or [payment negotiation](/docs/integration) to understand how it connects to your API. Features still being completed are marked on their pages and in the [service roadmap](/docs/roadmap).

## How a protected payment works

1. **Agree the work.** Define delivery criteria, pricing, budget, deadlines, arbiters and the fallback if arbitration times out.
2. **Fund the agreement.** The buyer funds the work budget and any accepted protocol and arbitration reserves. Both parties authorize the exact terms.
3. **Record delivery.** The seller submits a claim for completed work. Each claim reserves its own part of the budget and starts a review window.
4. **Review or dispute.** The buyer accepts the claim, or either party opens a dispute before review closes. An undisputed claim becomes eligible for settlement after review.
5. **Settle and recover unused funds.** Acceptance, review expiry, a valid ruling or the agreed fallback finalizes each claim. Unused funds return without spending another claim's reservation.

The contract controls fund allocation. Nyquist402 services provide transaction execution, private evidence delivery and payment history; they cannot change the funded agreement.

## Try it on testnet

The demo uses Circle's Stellar testnet USDC. Connect a testnet wallet, add the USDC trustline and follow one payment through the Buyer, Seller and Arbiter views. See [testnet setup](/docs/setup) for the network and contract details.

## Basic auth-capture

For payments that only need a spending ceiling and capture of the actual cost, [basic auth-capture](/docs/basic) provides a separate flow. Choose protected payments when deliveries need review and claim-level arbitration.
